# Nuclear strike

English · [Русский](ru/nuke.md)

A 1 or 15 kt nuclear warhead on an ICBM, a cruise missile or a B-2 bomb: flash, mushroom cloud, blast wave, fires, and
with a ground burst a crater and a fallout trail. The radii match a real explosion, with one block equal to one meter.

## How to launch

By default only operators can.

1. You need an ICBM item, or a missile or bomb plus a Nuclear Warhead ([recipes](munitions.md#recipes)).
2. On the console: the Nuclear weapon, or the "Warhead: ☢ nuclear" button of the Missile and the Bomb; yield 1 or
   15 kt; for the ICBM and the missile an air burst (wider destruction) or a ground burst (crater and fallout trail).
   The bomb explodes underground.
3. LMB in the binoculars or FIRE. A 3-second countdown follows, and LMB again cancels it.

The ICBM lifts off behind you and flies for 90 seconds.

If the world owner has allowed nuclear weapons for everyone, a player without operator rights needs a second key
whenever someone else is online: another player within 16 blocks presses `.` within 30 seconds. Every player is told
about such a launch, and it cannot be called off. The alarm at the target lasts at least 90 seconds: a missile or B-2
with a nuclear warhead takes off 90 seconds after the alarm starts.

## What happens

![Mushroom cloud over a town, the dust wall moving out below](img/nuke.jpg)

Everyone within 20 km of the target hears a siren in advance. Then come the flash, the fireball and the mushroom cloud.
The blast wave itself is invisible. The wind right behind it lifts what lies on the ground (dust and earth, smoke over a
burning town, spray over water) and rolls it forward as a churning wall, like a dust storm. At 15 kt the wall is about
150 m tall 1 km from the burst and about 50 m tall 4 km out; farther away it thins out and fades at about 9 km, where
the wind is too weak to lift dust. Far away, and more so in rain, the wall fades into the haze, like the mushroom cloud.
In its first seconds the fireball lights the top of the wall orange, even by day.
The wave breaks buildings as it reaches them: their pieces fly with the dust. The
ground shakes harder and harder as the wall comes closer. When the wall reaches you, it throws
your view away from the burst, and the wind behind it keeps rocking you. After a ground burst a short jolt comes through
the ground first, seconds before the wave.

The fireball shines white with brighter and darker patches boiling over it, and turns yellow and orange as it cools. It
rises and rolls into a ring of hot gas: the cap of the mushroom cloud, the same body from the first moment. As it rolls
up, its outside mixes with cold air, turns dark red and then into brown smoke. Dust in front of the fireball hides it,
and its light falls on the dust and smoke around it. By day it outshines the sky only for its first few seconds; at
night its red glow lasts a few seconds longer. The cap is a dense cloud of round billows, reddish brown at first from
the nitrogen oxides in it, then grey-white within about a minute as water condenses in it. While the cloud climbs, the
cap keeps rolling: gas goes up through its middle, out over the top and down the outside, and billows swell out of it
and sink back. The faster the cloud climbs, the faster it rolls; when the cloud stops rising, the cap stops too. The sun
lights one side of it, and the folds between the billows stay darker. A column of dust rises from the ground into the
cap, and a skirt of dust spreads around its foot. A burning town lights the foot of the column orange from below, and
the cap high above only faintly. Far away, and more so in rain, the cloud fades into the haze.

Behind the wave a pall of dust climbs over the town for a minute or two, up to about 200 m high at 15 kt and lower
toward the edge. From a rooftop near the blast you first see the wave cross the town, then the dust swallows you. At
night the fireball and the fires light it orange, as they light the top of the dust wall (the side of the wall away
from the fireball stays dark); its clumps are lighter on the side facing the light and darker in their folds. In the
thick of it you see only some 40 m and sounds are
muffled. The dust also fills the valleys around the town. It settles over a few minutes and drifts downwind. Where the
light of the fireball set the town on fire, a firestorm builds up over a few minutes. Columns of smoke rise over the
burning blocks, lean downwind and merge higher up into a ceiling of smoke over the town. At night the fires light it
orange-red from below, and the glow flickers; from a couple of kilometres away the town glows under its smoke, and the
dust around it catches the glow. A few times in the first ten minutes a fire whirl rises over the hottest fires for a
minute or so and wanders across them. From about the sixth minute the fires die down to smouldering, and by morning they
are out, together with the smoke. A server with nuclear fires turned off (`nuclear.fires`) has no firestorm and no smoke
haze over the town. Ash and scraps of burnt paper fall around you.

![Fireball and blast wave over a town](img/nuke-wave.jpg)

At 15 kt the wave kills out to about 0.4 km, the light of the fireball burns out to 3.4 km, and windows break out to
6 km. At 1 kt all of this is several times closer. A ground burst leaves a fallout trail downwind, with black rain.
Fallout starts to come down about 7 minutes after a 15 kt burst, and later farther downwind.

![A town after a 15 kt strike: ruins and fires](img/nuke-ruins.jpg)

The land around the burst is scorched. Near the centre the wave strips the turf down to bare dirt. Farther out the
light burns grass, flowers and crops in patches and leaves black char where it was strongest, and the grass, leaves and
water around turn yellow-brown and grey. The edge is ragged, not a ring. The scorched colours stay for good, also in
Distant Horizons terrain.

After an ICBM strike, Distant Horizons terrain shows the ruins of the hardest-hit area as the wave reaches them: the
server prepares them while the missile flies. At 15 kt the far edge of that area may show later. After a cruise
missile, a bomb or `/incoming nuke now` the distant ruins show some time after the wave. Broken windows and fallen trees
farther out show in the distant terrain once you come close.

The flash also marks what it lights. Wood, wool and hay that face the fireball char black in patches, and brick,
concrete, stone bricks and sandstone darken with soot. A player, a mob, an armor stand or a boat standing in the light
leaves a pale shadow of its shape on the wood or masonry behind it. These marks stay until the block is broken or
replaced. In the same instant lit lamps and substations of the [power grid](blackout.md) short out with sparks, and
grass, leaves and wood in the light start to smoke before the wave comes.

Over water the wave raises no dust. It runs across the water as a dark ring of ripples, with a wall of spray behind it
where the wind is strong, and white foam near the centre. Behind the wave the air over the water fills with white spray
instead of a dust pall. It thins within a minute and is gone in about three. Dust that the wind carries from the shore
out over the water stays brown, and spray carried ashore stays white. A burst on the water or just above it throws up
a hollow column of water, about a kilometre high at 15 kt within a few seconds. Its top opens into a crown of spray,
and the rising mushroom cloud covers it after about ten seconds. Right behind the wave a thin white cloud of
condensation hangs over the water for a second or two. The column then narrows and falls back into the water over
about twenty seconds, and the falling water rolls out as a white base surge for minutes. It is a bright mist: in its
thick part you see only some 50 m, and it thins out over a quarter of an hour.

Far away the blast comes as a sharp double crack and a deep boom, and then echoes back from the hills, forests and
towns around you: every slope that faces both the burst and you sends it back, later the farther it is. Animals around
the burst go quiet for a few minutes. Indoors the echoes are as muffled as the blast itself. Mobs run away from the
flash and the blast wave, then calm down; tamed, named and leashed mobs stay put, and mobs under cover don't run from
the wave. The town keeps burning for minutes: you hear the fires roar from where they burn, wind pulled into the fire
near them, ruins collapsing nearby, and now and then an explosion in the burning blocks.

## How to survive

- **Get underground.** The wave leaves natural ground alone: caves and bunkers stand, and earth stops both the light
  and the radiation.
- **Do not look at the fireball.** Its light kills and burns only those under open sky who can see it.
- **If you see a flash in the distance**, you have seconds before the wave arrives: get behind a slope, into a
  basement, into a pit.
- **Leave the fallout trail.** Water washes the dust off you (rain does not). Radiation sickness starts later, and milk
  does not cure it.

A Geiger Counter in hand clicks and shows your dose. Operators give it out.
